How Does Trade Competition with a Technological Leader Reshape Domestic Innovation? Evidence from South Korea

Sole-authored · Draft

Abstract

I study how import competition from a technological leader affects both the level and direction of innovation in a catching-up economy. I exploit South Korea’s abolition of product-specific restrictions on Japanese imports, which sharply increased imports from Japan but not from elsewhere. Patenting in exposed technologies declined before domestic production, suggesting that the innovation response was not driven solely by contemporaneous production losses. I construct a bilateral technology-gap measure from pre-liberalization Japanese and Korean patenting and show that the decline was larger where Japan’s initial technological advantage was greater. Most of the decline reflected reallocation across technologies. Broadly diversified incumbents shifted toward established alternatives, whereas technologically narrower incumbents reduced total patenting. Trade liberalization can therefore induce selective technological retreat without an equally large decline in incumbents’ overall patenting.

From Ad Avoidance to Ad Acceptance: Sponsored Listings and Consumer Inference

Sole-authored · Draft

Abstract

Whether consumers avoid or accept sponsored listings depends on what sponsorship reveals about seller quality. Standard advertising models emphasize quality signaling, whereby higher-quality sellers are more willing to advertise, but that logic alone cannot explain why sponsorship is sometimes read as bad news. Sponsored placement, however, serves a second function distinctive to sponsored search: it allows sellers denied organic exposure to obtain visibility through paid placement. I develop a model in which signaling and bypassing jointly determine selection into sponsorship. When the organic algorithm is reliable, sponsorship reveals an informative denial of organic display; as the algorithm becomes noisier, this adverse inference weakens. The sponsored posterior therefore crosses the organic posterior exactly once. Noise also raises the quality of sponsored listings while worsening the entire displayed-quality distribution. Nevertheless, because it raises advertising revenue per potential participant, the platform tolerates more noise when participation is less sensitive to displayed quality.
