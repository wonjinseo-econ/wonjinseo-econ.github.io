How Does Trade Competition with a Technological Leader Reshape Domestic Innovation? Evidence from South Korea

Sole-authored · Draft

Abstract

I study how import competition from a technological leader reshapes innovation in a catching-up economy. South Korea’s abolition of restrictions on Japanese imports generated a Japan-specific import surge and reduced patenting in exposed technologies, especially where Japan was initially further ahead. Broadly diversified incumbents reallocated patenting away from exposed technologies, whereas narrower incumbents experienced substantial overall contraction. Broad firms redirected patenting toward technologically related fields with greater technological opportunities. Import competition can therefore reshape innovation through selective technological retreat, discouraging research in affected fields while redirecting inventive activity toward alternative technological opportunities.

Premium or Penalty? Consumer Inference from the Sponsored Label

Sole-authored · Draft

Abstract

The same sponsored label can make a listing less attractive to consumers in some markets and more attractive in others. I develop a model in which sponsored placement serves both to signal quality and to provide visibility to sellers passed over by the organic algorithm. When both forces operate, consumers favor organic listings under a precise algorithm but sponsored listings under a noisy one. Algorithm noise also shapes the quality distribution of listings shown to consumers: greater noise improves the sponsored pool but shifts the overall quality distribution downward in the sense of first-order stochastic dominance. When the platform chooses algorithm noise, it trades advertising revenue against consumer participation. This tradeoff has implications for mandatory sponsorship disclosure: disclosure improves consumer inference from the sponsored label, but can also strengthen the platform’s incentive to degrade organic search, offsetting that benefit.
