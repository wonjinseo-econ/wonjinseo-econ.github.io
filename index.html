Working papers

How Does Trade Competition with a Technological Leader Reshape Domestic Innovation? Evidence from South Korea

Sole-authored · Draft coming soon

Abstract

Firms facing import competition may change not only how much they innovate but also where. Korea’s abolition of Japan-specific import restrictions on selected products exposed domestic firms to competition from a technological leader, raising imports of these products from Japan by 66 percent. At the technology level, patenting in exposed fields fell by 19 percent, with larger declines where Japan was initially further ahead. Yet firm-level evidence reveals substantial reallocation across technologies, with little change in incumbents’ total patenting. Firms with more prior experience outside the affected fields reallocated more strongly and sustained total patenting; those concentrated in and around the exposed technologies reallocated less and gradually reduced total patenting. The reallocation favored technologies that had been gaining momentum abroad before liberalization.

Premium or Penalty? Consumer Inference from the Sponsored Label

Sole-authored · Under review

Abstract

The “Sponsored” label can make a listing less attractive to consumers in some markets and more attractive in others. I develop a model in which sponsored placement serves both to signal quality and to provide visibility to sellers passed over by the organic algorithm. When both forces operate, consumers favor organic listings under a precise algorithm but sponsored listings under a noisy one. Algorithm noise also shapes the quality distribution of listings shown to consumers: greater noise improves the sponsored pool but shifts the overall quality distribution downward in the sense of first-order stochastic dominance. When choosing algorithm noise, the platform has an incentive to monetize the sponsored label’s informational value. This incentive has policy consequences: mandatory sponsorship disclosure can backfire by inducing greater algorithm noise.

Work in progress

Generative AI and the Restructuring of Digital Competition

with Paul S. Koh · Funded by the Yonsei Institute for AI and Social Innovation

Summary

This project studies how consumers adopt generative AI apps and how AI use substitutes for or complements existing digital services. Using a panel linking daily smartphone app usage with card-payment records for nearly 50,000 Korean consumers from 2022 onward, we separate free usage from paid subscriptions and track shifts in time allocation and spending across apps.

Identification and Estimation of Discrete Choice Models with a Fixed Latent Information Structure

with Paul S. Koh

Summary

This paper studies identification and counterfactual prediction in discrete choice when agents’ latent information is unobserved but fixed across markets. We characterize the resulting cross-market restrictions under a continuous prior, yielding an exact finite linear program for scalar binary choice. We also develop scalable outer approximations and confidence regions for general settings.

The Production of Artificial Intelligence: Evidence from Korean AI Firms

with Paul S. Koh and Devesh Raval

Summary

Using a linked panel of Korean AI firms, we study how labor, R&D, and computing resources shape AI production. Relative to manufacturing, AI firms exhibit higher R&D intensity and substantially greater dispersion in estimated revenue productivity. This pattern also holds against other venture firms in sales per worker. Industry sales are disproportionately concentrated among high-productivity firms.
