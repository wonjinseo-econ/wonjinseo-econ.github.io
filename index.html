How Does Trade Competition with a Technological Leader Reshape Domestic Innovation? Evidence from South Korea

Sole-authored · Draft

Abstract

Firms facing import competition may change not only how much they innovate but also which technologies they develop. Using South Korea’s abolition of import restrictions on Japanese goods, I show that liberalization increased Japanese imports and sharply reduced domestic patenting in exposed technologies, especially where Japan was initially further ahead. Among incumbent firms, the decline primarily reflected a shift in patenting away from exposed technologies rather than a reduction in total patenting. Firms with a larger share of prior patenting outside the affected fields maintained their total patenting while shifting toward other fields. By contrast, firms whose prior patenting was concentrated in and around the exposed technologies shifted less and experienced a gradual decline in total patenting. The shift favored technologies in which patenting abroad had been growing faster before liberalization. These findings show how competition from a technological leader reshapes the direction of domestic innovation, with firms’ innovation histories shaping their ability to redirect it.

Premium or Penalty? Consumer Inference from the Sponsored Label

Sole-authored · Draft

Abstract

The “Sponsored” label can make a listing less attractive to consumers in some markets and more attractive in others. I develop a model in which sponsored placement serves both to signal quality and to provide visibility to sellers passed over by the organic algorithm. When both forces operate, consumers favor organic listings under a precise algorithm but sponsored listings under a noisy one. Algorithm noise also shapes the quality distribution of listings shown to consumers: greater noise improves the sponsored pool but shifts the overall quality distribution downward in the sense of first-order stochastic dominance. When choosing algorithm noise, the platform has an incentive to monetize the sponsored label’s informational value. This incentive has policy consequences: mandatory sponsorship disclosure can backfire by inducing greater algorithm noise.

Work in progress

Generative AI and the Restructuring of Digital Competition

with Paul S. Koh

Summary

Studies the dynamics of consumer demand for generative AI apps, including adoption and subscription decisions, and how AI use reshapes demand for other digital services through changes in time allocation and spending. The project will link daily smartphone app usage with card-payment records for nearly 50,000 Korean consumers from 2022 onward, distinguishing initial adoption from paid subscription.

Identification and Estimation of Discrete Choice Models with a Fixed Latent Information Structure

with Paul S. Koh

Summary

Studies discrete choice when agents’ information is unobserved but assumed fixed across markets. Characterizes the additional identifying restrictions from common information and derives exact continuous-prior formulations, including a finite linear program for scalar binary choice. Develops scalable computation and confidence regions for structural parameters and counterfactual outcomes.

The Production of Artificial Intelligence: Evidence from Korean AI Firms

with Paul S. Koh and Devesh Raval

Summary

Uses linked firm-level microdata from the Korean Artificial Intelligence Industry Survey to study how specialized labor, R&D, accumulated knowledge, computing resources, and cloud use shape AI production and productivity. Examines cross-firm productivity dispersion and the roles of talent, intangible assets, and computing infrastructure.
