How Does Trade Competition with a Technological Leader Reshape Domestic Innovation? Evidence from South Korea

Sole-authored · Draft

Abstract

I study how import competition from a technological leader affects both the level and direction of innovation in a catching-up economy. I exploit South Korea’s abolition of product-specific restrictions on Japanese imports, which increased imports from Japan by 76% but not from elsewhere. In response, patenting in exposed technologies fell by 27%. The retreat followed two distinct patterns. First, using a bilateral technology-gap measure constructed from patenting data, I find larger declines where Japan was initially further ahead. Second, among incumbent firms, most of the decline reflected within-firm reallocation across technologies — exposed patenting fell by 31%, while total patenting fell by only 7%. Broadly diversified incumbents shifted toward other active fields, whereas less diversified ones reduced their total patenting. Trade liberalization can therefore induce selective technological retreat: distance from the leader shapes its extent, and firms’ scope for reallocation determines whether it translates into an overall decline in innovation.

From Ad Avoidance to Ad Acceptance: Bypassing and Signaling in Sponsored Search

Sole-authored · Draft

Abstract

The same sponsored label is bad news about seller quality in some markets and good news in others. I build a model in which two distinct motives for advertising jointly shape what sponsorship reveals about seller quality. Sellers advertise either to signal quality or to gain visibility when the organic algorithm passes them over. Consumers therefore infer whether a sponsored seller is signaling quality or bypassing the organic algorithm, with algorithm precision determining which motive dominates. As precision falls, sponsorship turns from bad news to good news about seller quality. But this improvement in sponsored quality comes at a broader cost: the entire distribution of displayed quality shifts downward. The platform chooses how much noise to allow by trading off higher consumer participation from better displayed quality against greater advertising revenue. Whether consumers accept or avoid sponsored results therefore depends on the reliability of organic search and reflects how responsive consumer participation is to displayed quality.
